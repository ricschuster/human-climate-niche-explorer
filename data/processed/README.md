# Processed data

Small files the site reads. All are generated by `scripts/` (see
`scripts/README.md`); do not edit them by hand. Rebuild with
`./scripts/build_all.sh`.

Shared conventions:

- `warming`: global warming above pre-industrial, degrees C. 1.0 is the
  2000-2020 observed climate (present day); 1.5 to 4.4 are the paper's levels,
  each taken from one SSP and 20-year period (`warming_levels.csv`).
- `population`: the fixed world population held constant across warming
  levels, as in Fig. 3: `6.9bn` (2010), `9.5bn` (SSP2 in 2070, the reference
  world for every slider-driven view), `11.1bn` (SSP3 in 2070).
- `metric`:
  - `hot`: people in places with mean annual temperature (MAT) above 29 C,
    "unprecedented heat". Recomputed from the authors' grid.
  - `outside_temp`: people left outside the niche by temperature change only.
  - `outside_combined`: people left outside the niche by temperature plus
    demographic change.
  The two `outside_*` metrics come from the authors' published per-model
  results; they are not recomputed here.
- `pct` is percent of the total population; `people` is a head count.
- Statistics across climate models: `mean`, `sd` (sample standard deviation,
  what the paper's text quotes as plus or minus), `p5` and `p95` (5th and
  95th percentiles, the shaded bands in the paper's figures), `min`, `max`,
  and `n_models`.

## Files

### `warming_levels.csv`

One row per warming level: `warming`, `ssp`, `period`, `policy` (label from
the paper: Paris Agreement goal, current policies, and so on), `n_models`,
`mat_variable` (source variable in `displacement_data.mat`).

### `by_warming_models.csv`

Fig. 3 points: one row per `population`, `warming`, `metric` and climate
`model` (numbered within each level), with `pct` and `people`.

### `by_warming_summary.csv`

The same, summarized per `population`, `warming`, `metric`: `pct_*` and
`people_*` statistics, `n_models`, `source`. This is what the headline numbers
use: for example, 9.5bn, 2.7 C, hot gives 22% or 2.1 billion.

### `by_warming_fit.csv` and `by_warming_fit_curve.csv`

The linear regressions drawn in Fig. 3, per `population` and `metric`:
`slope_pct_per_c`, `intercept_pct`, `r2`, `n`, `method`. The outside-niche
fits pass through zero at 0.3 C (the 1960-1990 reference), as in the authors'
code. The curve file has the fitted line (`fit`) and its 95% confidence band
(`lo`, `hi`) from 0 to 5 C in 0.05 C steps; draw it only over the range of the
data (1.0 to 4.4 C), since the hot-exposure line goes negative below about
1.2 C.

### `by_ssp_year_summary.csv`

Fig. 2: per `ssp` and `year` (centre of the 20-year period: 2030 is
2021-2040, ..., 2090 is 2081-2100) and `metric`, with the projected
`total_population` of that SSP and year, and `pct_*` and `people_*`
statistics. `ssp` is `Observed` for the 2010 baseline (2010 population,
2000-2020 climate). Populations change over time here, unlike the fixed
populations above.

### `niche_curves.csv`

Figs. 1a and 1c: population density against MAT. `curve` is `reference`
(1980 population, 1960-1990 climate: the niche itself), `current` (2010
population, 2000-2020 climate) or `warming` (a fixed `population` under the
MAT of a `warming` level). One row per 2 C wide MAT window: `mat` (centre),
`mat_lo`, `mat_hi`, and the `mean`, `p5`, `p95` across climate models.
Windows with fewer than 1000 grid cells are omitted. Values are relative
density (the paper's axis has no units); compare shapes, not absolute values.

### `countries_by_warming.csv`

Fig. 5 at every warming level. One row per country (Natural Earth admin 0)
and `warming`: `id` (Natural Earth `ADM0_A3`, joins to `countries.geojson`),
`iso3` (`-99` for areas without an ISO code), `name`, `continent`,
`exposed_9.5bn` and `exposed_11.1bn` (people in places above 29 C),
`population_9.5bn` and `population_11.1bn` (country totals),
`exposed_land_km2`, `country_area_km2`, `exposed_land_pct`, and
`paper_level` (true for 1.5 and 2.7 C, the levels the paper's Fig. 5 shows).

These are computed with the paper's method but current borders, so they
differ slightly from the paper even at 1.5 and 2.7 C (mainly Sudan, which in
the paper includes South Sudan). Levels other than 1.5 and 2.7 C are not in
the paper: label them as derived. Country totals use the ensemble-mean MAT,
so they do not add up to the global `hot` numbers, which average per-model
results (at 2.7 C: 1,994 million in countries vs. 2.1 billion globally).

### `countries.geojson`

Simplified Natural Earth country shapes for the map (about 5 km tolerance).
Properties: `id`, `name`. A few tiny territories have no shape after
simplification but still have rows in the table.

### `map/`

Raster layers for MapLibre, already in Web Mercator. `layers.json` gives the
image corner `coordinates`, file names, legend classes and colours.

- `hot_<warming>.png`: places with ensemble-mean MAT above 29 C.
- `population_light.png`, `population_dark.png`: people per 1/12 degree cell
  (about 100 km2) in the 9.5 billion world, in 7 classes from the paper's
  Fig. 4 legend; one file per page theme.
